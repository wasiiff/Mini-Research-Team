NoSQL databases are a broad category: document stores (MongoDB), key-value stores (Redis), wide-column stores (Cassandra), and graph databases (Neo4j). Many NoSQL systems trade strict schema and ACID guarantees for flexibility and horizontal scalability. Document databases, for example, let you store JSON-like documents that can vary in shape between records, which is useful for evolving apps.

NoSQL shines when you need to scale horizontally across many machines, or when your data model is naturally hierarchical and fits a document structure. For high write throughput and denormalized queries, NoSQL data models often keep related data together, reducing the need for joins and enabling low-latency reads.

The tradeoffs include fewer built-in relational guarantees, and often eventual consistency models rather than strict strong consistency. This requires careful application-level design if you need strict invariants.
